# Olympic Village Planning

Planning an Olympic Village requires organizers to create temporary housing and services for thousands of athletes and team officials while also considering what will happen to the site after the Games. Location is an important part of the process because athletes need reliable transportation to [[olympic-venues/olympic-venues-planning|competition and training venues]]. Organizers must also plan for security, dining, medical care, recreation, accessibility, and the daily movement of a large international population within the village.

## Olympic Village Design

Olympic Village design combines residential housing with the facilities and infrastructure needed to support athletes throughout the Games. Villages can include apartment buildings, dining halls, medical centers, fitness and recreation areas, transportation hubs, and shared public spaces. The layout must allow athletes to move efficiently between housing and services while maintaining controlled access and security. Many modern villages are also designed around their planned use after the Olympics, allowing temporary Olympic functions to be removed while permanent buildings become part of the surrounding community.

### Completed Olympic Village

A completed Olympic Village operates as a temporary community during the Games. The Paris 2024 Olympic and Paralympic Village was developed across parts of Saint-Denis, Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine, and Île-Saint-Denis. During the Games, it accommodated around 17,000 athletes and officials. After the Olympics and Paralympics, the site was planned for conversion into a permanent neighborhood with housing, offices, shops, public spaces, and other community uses. This type of conversion shows how the design of a village can serve the immediate needs of the Games while also being planned around long-term use.
