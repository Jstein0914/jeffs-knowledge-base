# History of Olympic Villages

Olympic Villages developed as organizers looked for better ways to house athletes from many countries in one location during the Games. Before dedicated villages were established, athletes often stayed in hotels, schools, military barracks, or other accommodations spread around the host city. The introduction of purpose-built athlete housing changed this arrangement by creating a central residential area where competitors could live, eat, and interact throughout the Olympic Games.

## Origins of the Olympic Village

The idea of housing athletes together developed gradually during the early Olympic Games. Paris 1924 included temporary cabins near the Olympic Stadium for athletes, but the 1932 Los Angeles Games are generally recognized as introducing the first purpose-built Olympic Village. The Los Angeles village provided housing, dining, medical services, and other facilities in one location for male athletes. Female athletes stayed separately in a hotel because the village was not open to them.

### Development of the Modern Olympic Village

Olympic Villages became increasingly complex as the Games grew in size and the needs of athletes expanded. Modern villages are planned as temporary residential communities that can include apartments, dining facilities, medical services, recreation areas, transportation connections, and other support services. Housing thousands of athletes and team officials in one area also makes it easier for organizers to coordinate transportation, security, meals, and other daily operations during the Games. Many villages are now planned with a specific post-Olympic use, such as conversion into permanent housing after athletes leave.
