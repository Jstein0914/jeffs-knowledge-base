# Life in the Olympic Village

Life in the Olympic Village brings together athletes and team officials from many countries in a shared residential environment during the Games. Although athletes spend much of their time training, competing, and recovering, the village is where many of their daily activities take place. Athletes eat meals, receive services, spend time with teammates, and interact with competitors from other countries. This shared environment makes the village both an operational part of the Games and a unique part of the Olympic experience.

> “So we visited back and forth all the time, traded costumes, learned a few words of this and that...”  
> — Jean Shiley Newhouse

## Dining in the Olympic Village

Dining facilities must serve thousands of athletes with different nutritional needs, cultural preferences, and competition schedules. Large dining halls often operate for extended hours and provide a wide variety of foods so athletes can choose meals appropriate for their sport and personal needs. Organizers must also account for allergies, religious dietary requirements, and other restrictions. Because athletes may train or compete at different times throughout the day, dining services are designed to provide reliable access to meals throughout the Olympic schedule.

### Sleeping in the Olympic Village

Athlete housing in the Olympic Village is designed to provide a place to rest and recover between training and competition. Rooms are typically located in apartment-style residential buildings and shared by athletes from the same national delegation. Organizers provide beds, basic furnishings, climate control where appropriate, and access to nearby services. Sleep can be especially important during the Games because athletes may be adjusting to travel, time-zone changes, demanding competition schedules, and the activity of living alongside thousands of other Olympic participants.
