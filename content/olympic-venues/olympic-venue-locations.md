# Olympic Venue Locations

Olympic competitions are often spread across multiple locations rather than held entirely within the host city. Venue locations are selected based on existing facilities, transportation, available space, and the needs of a particular sport. Some events require environments that cannot be provided near the main Olympic area, while others may be placed outside the host city to make use of existing venues. As a result, an Olympic Games can involve a network of competition sites extending beyond the host city to other locations within the host nation.

## Summer Olympic Venue Locations

Summer Olympic venues can be distributed across a wide geographic area because different sports require different types of facilities and environments. Stadiums, arenas, pools, and indoor facilities may be concentrated near the host city, while sports such as sailing, football, and surfing can require competition sites farther away. During Paris 2024, for example, sailing events were held in Marseille, football matches were played in several cities across France, and surfing took place in Tahiti. These locations allowed organizers to use sites suited to the requirements of each sport.

### Winter Olympic Venue Locations

[[winter-games/history-of-the-winter-games|Winter Olympic venue locations]] are strongly influenced by terrain, elevation, snow conditions, and access to specialized facilities. Indoor ice sports may take place in or near a host city, while alpine skiing, ski jumping, sliding sports, and other outdoor events often require mountain locations farther away. Milano Cortina 2026 demonstrated this distributed approach, with events held across multiple areas in northern Italy rather than within a single host city. This allows organizers to use established winter-sport locations and facilities that meet the needs of different competitions.
