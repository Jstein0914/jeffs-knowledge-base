# Olympic Venues Planning

Planning Olympic venues begins years before the Games and requires host cities to determine where competitions will take place, which existing facilities can be used, and what new venues must be built. Organizers must consider the requirements of each sport along with transportation, security, spectator access, media operations, and connections to other Olympic facilities. Decisions about permanent and temporary venues are also important because they affect construction costs and how facilities may be used after the Games.

## Olympic Venue Design

Olympic venue design depends heavily on the needs of a particular sport. Competition areas must meet technical requirements while also providing space for athletes, officials, spectators, media, and event operations. Accessibility, transportation, security, and crowd movement also influence the layout of a venue. Some facilities are designed specifically for Olympic competition, while existing stadiums and arenas may be renovated or temporarily modified. Temporary seating and other removable structures can also expand a venue during the Games without creating permanent facilities that may not be needed afterward.

### Completed Venue

A completed Olympic venue brings together the competition space and the supporting areas required to operate an Olympic event. These can include athlete areas, spectator seating, media facilities, entrances, security checkpoints, transportation access, and spaces for officials and event staff. The final design depends on the sport and whether the venue is permanent, temporary, or adapted from an existing facility. Examining a completed venue can show how these different planning and design requirements work together in one location.
