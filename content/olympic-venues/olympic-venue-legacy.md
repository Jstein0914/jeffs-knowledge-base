# Olympic Venue Legacy

The legacy of an Olympic venue depends largely on what happens to it after the Games. Stadiums, arenas, aquatic centers, and other facilities can continue serving communities through professional and amateur sports, recreation, training, concerts, and other events. Some venues become permanent landmarks associated with a city's Olympic history. Others struggle to find regular uses once the temporary demand created by the Games is gone. Because of this, [post-Olympic use](https://www.queenelizabetholympicpark.co.uk/our-stories/legacy-london-2012-has-come-life-queen-elizabeth-olympic-park)) has become an important part of venue planning.

## Post-Olympic Venue Use

Host cities use different approaches to determine what happens to Olympic venues after competition ends. Existing facilities may simply return to their previous use, while newly constructed venues can be converted for community, professional, or recreational purposes. Temporary venues may be dismantled completely. Some permanent facilities are modified after the Games by reducing seating or changing interior spaces so they better match long-term needs. These decisions can affect maintenance costs, public access, and whether a venue remains useful for decades after the Olympics.

### Successful Reuse and Abandoned Venues

Olympic venue legacies vary considerably between host cities. London's 2012 Olympic Stadium was converted into a multipurpose venue and later became the home stadium of West Ham United. The Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum has remained in regular use and has hosted Olympic competition during two Games, with a third planned for 2028. In contrast, some venues built for the Athens 2004 Olympics became underused or fell into disuse in the years after the Games. These different outcomes demonstrate why organizers consider long-term use when deciding whether to construct, renovate, temporarily adapt, or reuse a venue.
