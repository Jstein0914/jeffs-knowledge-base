# Summer Team Sports

The first modern Summer Games in 1896 featured team events in gymnastics, including the parallel bars and horizontal bar team competitions, but the official Olympic program of sports and events did not yet include the major team sports associated with the Summer Games today. Team sports were added as the Games developed. Football (soccer) and water polo appeared in 1900, field hockey in 1908, basketball in 1936, and volleyball in 1964.

## Team Competition in the Summer Olympics

[[summer-olympic-competition|Olympic team competitions]] use different formats depending on the sport and event. Many begin with teams divided into groups, where they play a series of games to determine which ones move forward. Basketball and soccer, for example, use group play followed by elimination rounds that lead to the medal games. Beach volleyball can also use a Lucky Loser playoff, where eligible third-place teams compete for the remaining places in the Round of 16. Water polo also uses classification matches to determine final placements for teams no longer competing for a medal.

### Major Olympic Team Sports

The Summer Games include team sports that differ in the number of players, playing surface, equipment, and basic style of play. Major Olympic team sports include:

- **Basketball:** five players from each team are on the court at a time, with teams scoring by shooting the ball through the opponent's basket.
- **Soccer:** eleven players from each team are on the field, with teams attempting to score in the opponent's goal.
- **Volleyball:** includes indoor volleyball, played by six players per team on the court, and beach volleyball, played by teams of two.
- **Water polo:** seven players per team are in the pool at a time, including six field players and one goalkeeper, combining swimming, passing, and shooting at a goal.
- **Field hockey:** eleven players per team compete on the field using sticks to move the ball and score in the opponent's goal.
- **Handball:** seven players per team are on the court, with players passing and throwing the ball into the opponent's goal.
- **Rugby sevens:** seven players per team compete in a faster, shorter version of rugby union.
