# History of the Summer Games

The Summer Olympic Games trace their origins to ancient Greece, where athletic festivals were held for centuries as part of religious and cultural tradition. When the modern Olympic movement began in the late 1800s, organizers looked to these ancient Games as inspiration for creating an international sporting event. Athens was chosen to host the first modern Games in 1896 to honor the Olympics’ historical roots. That inaugural competition featured 241 male athletes from 14 nations competing in nine sports, a much smaller scale than today’s Olympics.

## Origins and Development of the Modern Summer Olympics

Pierre de Coubertin led the effort to revive the Olympics, helping establish the International Olympic Committee in 1894.

> “When I restored the Olympiads, I did not look to what was nearby; I looked to the far-off future. I wanted to give the world, in an enduring way, an ancient institution whose guiding principle was becoming necessary for its health.”
>
> — Pierre de Coubertin

Over time, the Summer Games expanded by adding more countries, athletes, and events. Women’s participation was gradually introduced, and the Olympic program evolved as [[summer-olympic-sports|sports were added or removed]]. As the Olympics developed, traditions such as opening ceremonies, national representation, and medal awards became defining features of the modern Games.

### Major Historical Events of the Summer Games

The Summer Olympics have been affected by major historical events throughout their history, including events that became important [[major-achievements/social-and-political-milestones|social and political milestones]]. Wars, political conflicts, acts of terrorism, and even a global pandemic have interrupted or changed the Games. Some notable examples include:

- **1916, 1940, and 1944 Canceled Games:** The Summer Olympics were canceled during World War I and World War II.
- **1936 Berlin Games:** The Olympics were held in Nazi Germany under Adolf Hitler, where the Nazi government used the Games to promote its image to an international audience.
- **1972 Munich Games:** Members of the Israeli Olympic team were taken hostage during a terrorist attack that resulted in the deaths of 11 Israeli team members. These were also the first Summer Olympics I remember watching.
- **1980 and 1984 Olympic Boycotts:** The United States led a boycott of the 1980 Moscow Games following the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan. Four years later, the Soviet Union led a boycott of the 1984 Los Angeles Games.
- **2020 Tokyo Games:** The COVID-19 pandemic caused the Games to be postponed until 2021, the first postponement of the Olympics rather than a cancellation.
