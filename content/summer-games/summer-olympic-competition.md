# Summer Olympic Competition

Competing in the Summer Games begins before an athlete or team arrives at the Olympics. Athletes must first earn a place through the qualification system established for their sport. Once at the Games, they compete according to the event or tournament structure of their sport, with the goal of reaching a medal position. Although the path differs greatly between sports, qualification, Olympic competition, and medal results form the basic progression from earning a place at the Games to reaching the Olympic podium.

## Qualifying for the Summer Olympics

Qualification for the Summer Games differs by sport. Each sport's International Federation develops its qualification system, which is subject to approval by the International Olympic Committee (IOC). Athletes and teams may earn places through international qualifying events, world rankings, qualifying standards, or designated tournaments. Countries are also limited in how many competitors they can enter in certain events. In some sports, earning a qualifying position does not automatically determine which athlete will compete, because National Olympic Committees and national sport federations may use their own selection processes to choose the athletes who represent their country.

### Medals and the Olympic Podium

Gold, silver, and bronze medals are generally awarded to the top three finishers in an Olympic event, but not every sport awards medals in exactly the same way. Some combat sports, including boxing, judo, taekwondo, and wrestling, award two bronze medals in each event. Ties can also change the medal results, depending on the rules of the sport. During the medal ceremony, the medalists take their places on the podium, the flags of their countries are raised, and the national anthem of the gold medalist's country  is played.
