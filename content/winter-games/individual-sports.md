# Individual Sports

The first Winter Games in Chamonix in 1924 included many sports centered on individual competition, including figure skating, speed skating, ski jumping, cross-country skiing, and Nordic combined. As the Winter Games developed, the Olympic program expanded to include additional individual sports and disciplines. Alpine skiing made its Olympic debut in 1936, biathlon followed in 1960, and luge was added in 1964. More recently, short track speed skating and freestyle skiing joined the program in 1992, snowboarding debuted in 1998, and ski mountaineering made its Olympic debut at Milano Cortina 2026.

## Individual Competition in the Winter Olympics

Individual Olympic competitions use different formats depending on the sport and event. Alpine skiing and speed skating use timed races, where athletes compete to record the fastest time. Figure skating and freestyle skiing include events in which judges score performances based on established criteria such as difficulty and execution. Ski jumping combines points for distance and style, while biathlon combines cross-country skiing with rifle shooting and applies penalties for missed targets.

### Major Olympic Individual Sports

The Winter Games include individual sports that differ in the skills required, equipment used, playing environment, and basic style of competition. Major Olympic individual sports include:

- **Alpine skiing:** skiers race downhill courses in events that test speed and technical ability, including downhill, super-G, giant slalom, and slalom.
- **Cross-country skiing:** skiers race over snow-covered courses that test endurance, speed, and technique across different distances.
- **Figure skating:** skaters perform jumps, spins, steps, and other elements on ice, with performances evaluated by judges.
- **Speed skating:** skaters race against the clock on an oval ice track over different distances.
- **Ski jumping:** athletes ski down a ramp and launch from a takeoff point, with results determined by factors including distance and style.
- **Biathlon:** athletes combine cross-country skiing with rifle shooting, requiring both endurance and accuracy.
- **Snowboarding:** athletes ride a snowboard in events that can involve racing, aerial tricks, or judged runs on courses and features designed for different forms of competition.
- **Luge:** athletes race individually on a small sled down a refrigerated track, with results determined by time.
- **Skeleton:** athletes race headfirst on a small sled down an ice track, with the fastest combined times determining placement.
