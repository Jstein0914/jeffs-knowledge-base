# Winter Olympic Competition

Competition at the Winter Games brings together athletes from around the world in sports contested on snow and ice. The number and type of events have expanded considerably since the first Winter Games in Chamonix in 1924. Athletes may compete individually, as part of a team, or in events that combine individual performances into a team result. Competition formats vary by sport and can include timed races, judged performances, head-to-head matches, elimination rounds, and events that combine multiple skills.

## Qualification for the Winter Games

Qualification for the Winter Games differs by sport, with each sport using an established qualification system. Athletes and teams may earn places through international qualifying events, world rankings, qualifying standards, or designated tournaments. Countries are also limited in the number of athletes they can enter in many events. These systems determine which athletes and teams are eligible to compete while keeping the overall number of Olympic participants within the available athlete quotas.

### Competition Formats and Medal Events

Winter Olympic medal events use different competition formats based on the sport. Timed sports such as alpine skiing, speed skating, luge, and skeleton determine results through finishing times. Figure skating and some freestyle skiing and snowboarding events use judging systems that evaluate elements such as difficulty and execution. Ice hockey and curling use preliminary competition followed by rounds that determine which teams advance toward the medal games. Other sports use combinations of formats, such as ski jumping, where athletes receive points based on distance and style. Despite these differences, each event concludes with gold, silver, and bronze medals awarded to the top finishers.
