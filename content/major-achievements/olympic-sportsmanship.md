# Olympic Sportsmanship

Some Olympic achievements are remembered because athletes place fairness, compassion, or another competitor’s well-being ahead of their own competitive interests. At Seoul 1988, Canadian sailor Lawrence Lemieux interrupted his race to assist competitors whose boat had capsized, sacrificing his own position in the event. Other examples have involved athletes helping injured competitors or giving rivals the opportunity to compete fairly. These moments can become especially significant at the Olympics, where athletes may have spent years preparing for only a few opportunities to compete.

## Acts of Selflessness

At Innsbruck 1964, Italian bobsledder Eugenio Monti helped British rivals Tony Nash and Robin Dixon after a bolt on their sled broke. Monti provided equipment that allowed the British team to continue competing, and Nash and Dixon went on to win the two-man bobsled gold medal while Monti and his teammate finished with bronze. Monti’s decision became a notable example of sportsmanship because he helped direct rivals competing for the same medals.

### Respect Between Competitors

At Tokyo 2020, high jumpers [Mutaz Essa Barshim](https://worldathletics.org/news/feature/barshim-tamberi-friendship-tokyo-olympics-gold) of Qatar and Gianmarco Tamberi of Italy remained tied after both cleared 2.37 meters and failed at 2.39 meters. The competition could have continued with a jump-off, but the two athletes agreed to share the Olympic gold medal instead. 

> **“Can we have two golds?” — Mutaz Essa Barshim**

Their decision became a memorable moment between two rivals who had been competing against one another for the same championship.
