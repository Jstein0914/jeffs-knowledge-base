# Innovation and Athletic Performance

Olympic achievement can involve changing how athletes compete as well as winning medals or setting records. At Mexico City 1968, American high jumper Dick Fosbury won gold using the unconventional backward technique that became known as the Fosbury Flop.

His technique changed the approach to high jumping and was eventually adopted by athletes throughout the sport. Olympic performance has also been influenced by developments in training, equipment, clothing, and technology. Some innovations become standard parts of competition, while others are regulated or restricted when they raise concerns about fairness or competitive advantage.

## Advances in Training and Performance

Training methods have changed as athletes and coaches have developed new approaches to conditioning, nutrition, recovery, and technique. Czechoslovak distance runner Emil Zátopek became known for his demanding use of interval training when the method was still considered unusual. Modern athletes can also use biomechanics, video analysis, and workload monitoring to study their performance and adjust their preparation. These tools allow training to be tailored more closely to an athlete’s technique, recovery, and individual needs.

### Technology and Equipment Innovation

Changes in equipment and technology can also affect Olympic performance. Full-body polyurethane swimsuits became a prominent example after swimmers using high-tech suits produced a wave of record-breaking performances in 2008 and 2009. Swimming’s governing body responded with new swimsuit regulations that took effect in 2010. Other innovations, including clap skates in speed skating and carbon-plated running shoes, have changed the equipment athletes use to compete. Electronic timing and photo-finish systems have also changed how performances and close finishes are measured.
