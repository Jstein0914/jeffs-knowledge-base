# Olympic Underdogs

Olympic underdogs are athletes or teams who face significant disadvantages compared with their competitors. These can include limited funding, inadequate facilities or equipment, inexperience, or coming from a country with little history in a particular sport. The Jamaican bobsled team at [[olympic-venues/olympic-venue-locations|Calgary 1988]] became one of the best-known examples when athletes from a tropical country with no established bobsled tradition competed at the Winter Olympics. Without the training facilities and established bobsled program available to many of their competitors, reaching the Olympics was itself a significant accomplishment for the team.

## Unexpected Olympic Triumphs

Some underdogs go beyond reaching the Olympics and produce an unexpected victory. At Lake Placid 1980, the U.S. men's hockey team defeated the heavily favored Soviet team, which had dominated international hockey. The American roster consisted largely of younger players with much less international experience. The famous “Miracle on Ice” was not the gold-medal game, however. The United States still had to defeat Finland afterward to win the gold medal.

### Overcoming Adversity

Olympic athletes have reached the Games after overcoming very different circumstances. British ski jumper Eddie “The Eagle” Edwards competed at Calgary 1988 despite having far fewer resources and training opportunities than established ski jumpers. He finished last in both events, but became one of the best-known athletes of those Olympics. Syrian swimmer Yusra Mardini faced a much different challenge after fleeing the war in Syria and eventually settling in Germany as a refugee. She later competed at Rio 2016 as a member of the first IOC Refugee Olympic Team and returned for Tokyo 2020. Their experiences show how reaching the Olympics can represent an achievement beyond an athlete’s final result.
